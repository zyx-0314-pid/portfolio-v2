Specialized Job Fit Assessment Platform

JFP focuses specifically on Job Fit assessment preparation and scheduling. Rather than reproducing the wider account, candidate, reporting, and administration responsibilities of GoPick or AC Local, JFP provides a narrower workflow for configuring and initiating Job Fit assessments.

The Job Fit model combines three assessment dimensions—Cognitive Ability, Personality, and Occupational Interest—with benchmark, norm, competency, and scheduling configuration before a candidate is handed into the broader assessment runtime.

Across the People Dynamics product lineup, JFP serves two distinct platform demographics: JFP – GoPick (international platform adhering to stricter regulatory standards such as GDPR and global data compliance) and JFP – AC Local (Philippine local platform tailored to domestic organizational needs).

Job Fit Profile Dimensions

  • Cognitive Ability Problem-solving capacity, spatial reasoning, verbal logic, and analytical evaluation.
  • Personality Behavioral traits, workplace tendencies, interpersonal style, and emotional resilience.
  • Occupational Interest Career orientation, role affinity, work environment alignment, and task motivations.

My Role & Ownership Boundary

As a Full-Stack Engineer working on JFP, my verified contributions center on modernizing the schedule-test workflow, introducing clearer controller/service/repository/model/view boundaries, improving candidate staging and validation, strengthening account-selection guardrails, supporting candidate import workflows, and isolating Job Fit-specific assessment queries.

This case study does not claim ownership of the original JFP product, proprietary assessment methodology, scoring engine, norms content, report-generation algorithms, or the complete candidate assessment runtime. It focuses only on application and integration work supported by implementation and commit evidence.

JFP Within the Assessment Ecosystem

JFP operates as a specialized application adjacent to the broader assessment platforms rather than as a general-purpose replacement for them. Its responsibility is concentrated around Job Fit configuration and scheduling, while the parent ecosystem owns broader account, candidate, assessment-runtime, results, and reporting responsibilities.

Note: Deployment-specific differences between the international (GoPick / GDPR rules) and Philippine-local (AC Local) variants are intentionally excluded where they are not technically verified.

Mixed Legacy Application Moving Toward Explicit Layers

JFP remains a mixed architecture. Long-lived flat PHP scripts, static SQL models, and page-level orchestration continue to coexist with newer schedule-test modules organized around explicit Controller, Service, Repository, Model, and View responsibilities.

Modernization therefore follows the same risk-controlled principle used elsewhere in the ecosystem: touched behavior is moved toward explicit boundaries while stable legacy areas remain operational until future work requires modification.

Schedule-Test Modernization

01 — Page Orchestration

Before

Scheduling behavior was distributed across schedule_test.php, section includes, markup, and page-level execution logic.

Change

Extracted schedule-test orchestration into dedicated controller and service boundaries with reusable models, views, and components.

After

The controller coordinates the request while services build scheduling context and presentation components render the workflow.

02 — Job Fit Data Access

Before

Assessment-specific SQL and preset lookup behavior followed generic legacy scheduling patterns.

Change

Introduced a dedicated JobFitAssessmentRepository for JFP norms and Cognitive, Personality, and Occupational Interest preset queries.

After

Job Fit-specific persistence behavior now has a clearer location and named assessment boundary.

03 — Candidate Staging

Before

Candidate information moved directly through loosely validated form/import state.

Change

Introduced structured candidate models, services, session-backed staging, and import validation.

After

Candidate records can be reviewed, validated, imported, and removed before final persistent scheduling occurs.

Candidate Preparation Before Persistence

JFP deliberately separates candidate preparation from final schedule persistence. Candidate records are staged in session during the scheduling wizard so that users can validate, review, import, and remove entries before the system creates permanent assessment records.

Validation

Required candidate fields, valid email format, supported gender values, lengths, and import consistency are checked before scheduling.

Duplicate Handling

Import processing identifies invalid or repeated candidate rows before they can enter the final persistence flow.

Account Guardrails

Candidate-registration accounts are filtered using effective expiration rules, reducing the risk of scheduling against expired account contexts.

Account Locking

Once staged candidate records depend on the selected account, account changes are constrained to prevent context mismatches.

Atomic Cross-System Scheduling

Creating a JFP assessment schedule spans multiple related records across parent and tenant data boundaries. Partial success would create orphaned identities, candidates, schedules, or authorization records.

The scheduling workflow therefore executes the related writes transactionally and treats individual insert failure as a failure of the complete operation, rolling back rather than leaving partially created assessment state.

Technical Decisions

1. Keep JFP as a Specialized Application

Context: Job Fit configuration requires workflows different from general account and candidate management.

Decision: Preserve JFP as a focused application integrated with the broader assessment ecosystem.

Benefit: Keeps specialized assessment configuration isolated.

Tradeoff: Requires explicit integration contracts with parent databases and session context.

2. Direct Parent-Database Integration

Context: Parent platforms already own accounts, users, candidates, schedules, and assessment execution.

Decision: Integrate JFP through existing main and tenant database contracts.

Benefit: Immediate interoperability with the established platform.

Tradeoff: Strong coupling to existing schema structures.

3. Session-Backed Candidate Staging

Context: Schedulers may prepare multiple candidates before committing assessment records.

Decision: Stage validated candidates in session until final submission.

Benefit: Allows review/import/removal without premature persistent writes.

Tradeoff: Session state requires explicit consistency and validation controls.

4. Incremental 5-Layer Modernization

Context: JFP is an active legacy PHP application and cannot be cleanly rewritten all at once.

Decision: Refactor touched scheduling behavior into Controller → Service → Repository → Model → View boundaries.

Benefit: Improves traceability while preserving working legacy behavior.

Tradeoff: Mixed architectural patterns remain during transition.

Verified Qualitative Outcomes

Explicit Job Fit Data Boundary

Safer Candidate Preparation

Stronger Account Guardrails

Atomic Schedule Creation

Related user, candidate, metadata, schedule, and authorization writes are treated as a single transactional operation rather than independent best-effort inserts.

Key Engineering Takeaway

Connected legacy applications are not made safer simply by separating them into different codebases. The important boundary is making ownership, state transfer, and persistence responsibilities explicit enough that developers can understand where one application ends and another begins.

JFP's modernization work reinforced that incremental architecture improvements are most valuable when applied around high-risk workflows—in this case, candidate preparation and cross-system assessment scheduling—rather than attempting broad restructuring without an immediate operational reason.
